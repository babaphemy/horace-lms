They must demonstrate the skill.

8. WEEKLY CLASS STRUCTURE
   For three sessions per week:
   Session 1 — Learn
   30% theory + 70% demonstration
   Instructor explains the concept and demonstrates the tools.

Session 2 — Practice
20% theory + 80% hands-on
Students perform guided exercises.

Session 3 — Challenge
10% theory + 90% problem-solving
Students receive a scenario with limited instructions.

For example:

"The company believes an employee's computer has been compromised. You are the security analyst. Investigate and determine what happened."
Students must figure out the process themselves.
This is where mentorship becomes much more valuable than conventional classroom teaching.

The most important design principle
Your center should measure success by what students can actually do, not how much content you covered.
At graduation, a student should be able to sit at a workstation and receive a scenario like:

"We believe our company has suffered a security incident. You have four hours to investigate, determine the likely attack path, identify affected systems, preserve evidence, recommend containment measures and present your findings to management."

"Which chapter of the textbook covers this?"
they should know how to approach the problem.
